# Candidate-region association study report

## Where the study stands
The discovery plate can carry a conclusion. Participant QC found **1** failed participant out of **240**, a failure fraction of **0.004166666666666667**, below the protocol limit of **0.1**. The study therefore proceeds rather than being re-genotyped.

Variant QC retained **137** of **150** variants and excluded **13**. Retention used the protocol call-rate, minor-allele-frequency, and unaffected-control HWE rules.

## Cohort comparability
The leading-axis screen did not show separation of affected and unaffected participants: PC1 p-value was **0.38345330708450176**. The groups can therefore be compared under the protocol; no ancestry covariate was required by this screen.

## Replication and reporting
The released replication batch contains **0** nominated candidates. Therefore the required replication comparison could not be run: there was no candidate list for which to evaluate the replication p-value and same-direction rule. The number of replicated reportable candidates is consequently not established, and no association can be reported as replicated.

## What changed, what it achieved, and what to do next
The analysis applied the protocol gates in order: participant QC, variant QC, cohort structure assessment, corrected discovery testing, and replication review. This established that the plate and retained cohort are usable and identified discovery candidates, but it did not establish any reportable association. The investigators should preserve the QC exclusions and analysis record, ask the data steward or study lead why no discovery candidates were nominated for replication, and rerun replication only after a pre-specified candidate list is released. Future reporting should require independent replication with p below **0.05** and the same effect direction.
